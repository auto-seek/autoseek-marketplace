# Filmmaking project

Request: $ARGUMENTS

Use get_filmmaking_project before changes. The returned project and revision are shared with the bound Filmmaking App; a chat outline is not a saved project. Keep approved creative choices and unrelated edits. Use the user's language for project text.

The same Team can retain several independent works. The snapshot lists works and identifies the active one; a missing project.workId is the legacy work. Use create_filmmaking_work with a name and the current revision to start a new work, or switch_filmmaking_work with a listed workId and current revision to resume one. Each work keeps its own script, shots, audio, subtitles, editing settings, exports and production progress. Team assets, characters and scenes are shared. Preserve the service-owned workId when updating the active project; never edit the catalog with file tools. Switching refuses active requests, unsettled fees and pending output. Do not cancel, retry or replace existing production merely to switch; paused unsubmitted plans remain available after switching back.

## Plan and maintain the project

Prefer native audiovisual video generation. Put exact dialogue, sound effects and music in the video prompt; preparation adds bound character voice.description and ordered picture purposes. Preserve dialogue verbatim in its original language, never invent lines, and respect explicit silence/no-music requests. Do not plan separate speech/music by default; use them only when the user asks or requests audio rework. Narration language follows explicit intent or dialogue/context; ask when uncertain, never assume Chinese. Text voice descriptions need no TTS voice ID. When the dialogue language is known, format supplied lines as `<d>[Language]verbatim dialogue</d>` in the video prompt. Preserve the words and language exactly; ask when the language is unclear instead of applying a fixed language default.

Dialogue disciplines (from aidrama's storyboard-to-video prompt contract; raise verbatim-hit rate):

- Single source of truth: write a shot's spoken line only in its caption; never restate it in description, motion or elsewhere. Preparation merges it into the `<d>` line. State a speaker only once per line.
- For a multi-character shot with dialogue, add "non-speakers keep their lips still". Dialogue is sound only: it must not trigger subtitles, flashbacks or visualized speech. Keep fixed negative terms: no subtitles, no logo, no watermark, no UI, no duplicate/split-self of a character.
- A shot without dialogue is not silent void: write "no dialogue, keep ambient sound (name it, e.g. room tone, rain)" so adjacent shots stay sonically continuous.
- Face protection for any shot with dialogue or a visible speaking face: the same face persists across the film; speech uses only small natural mouth movement and slow small head turns; forbid face stretching, melting, feature drift, eye-size change, mouth misalignment and near-duplicate faces.
- Performance and dialogue timeline calibration: avoid stacking conflicting emotion labels at once (such as "crying angrily"); progress through a protective-layer arc (_restraint / defensive mask → trigger word or event cracks the mask → brief outburst or vocal break → recovery or tear release_) anchored to each beat's dramatic objective (counterattack, self-justification, concealment, crack, confession). For close-ups, decompose facial action into five zones — brows (`AU1` inner raise, `AU4` lowerer), eyelids/gaze (`AU5` upper lid raise, `AU7` lid tightener, `<0.5s` gaze drop and return), nostrils (flare/tremor), lips (`AU23` tightener, `AU15` corner depressor, `AU25/AU26` parting on trigger words), and chin/throat (`AU17` chin raiser/tremor, swallow) — with `Onset → Apex → Offset` dynamics, physiological pauses (interrupted chest inhalation, parting lips without immediate sound, breathy first syllable attempt), and **immediate mouth closure and jaw re-tightening right after the final spoken word**. Write natural-language visible action first and attach minimal FACS AU codes (`A–E` intensity) only as calibration.
- Three-block constraints for a dialogue or high-risk shot: MUST KEEP = identity, wardrobe, screen axis, fixed space, continuous props; ONLY CHANGES THIS SHOT = the state changes this shot allows; FORBID = this shot's 1–3 most likely failure modes (including anti-preemption rules such as forbidding premature tears before the trigger line ends or frame-one roaring) plus the fixed negative terms above.

Image and video reference_sources accepts ordered {asset_id} or {step_id} entries; step sources must appear in dependsOn. Image 2.5 uses up to ten references, including bound shot/character/scene cards. H3 reference mode includes up to nine card references, labelled <Picture 1>, <Picture 2>, etc. New video plans use reference mode when references exist, otherwise text mode. For explicit frame control use first_frame (one image), last_frame (one image), or first_last_frame (exactly two images, first then last); frame modes use only the explicitly selected sources and do not append card images. Never drop images or truncate required dialogue.

Record the overall subject and visual style in brief.subject and brief.style. Keep the script/source and current written draft in brief.source and draft. For a missing project, create version 2 with an empty asset library, characters, scenes, shots and renders, aspectRatio 16:9, musicAssetId null and musicVolume 0.2; fill subject, style and source from the request. Use service "" and panels 1 unless the user asks otherwise; the retained service/panels brief fields support older drafts, not a mandatory grid.

Give characters and scenes stable IDs, names, descriptions and actual reference-image asset IDs. Before inventing a recurring character, check the account library with list_filmmaking_characters and reuse one with import_filmmaking_character at the current revision; edits, confirmation and locks stay with the owner in the App. Keep a flexible ordered shot list. Each shot has a stable ID, title, visual description, motion, caption, durationMs, trimStartMs, characterIds, sceneId, referenceAssetIds, candidateAssetIds, selectedAssetId and locked. The tool schema supplies limits. A planned shot may have no selected media yet; do not invent cloud IDs or pretend a prompt is a generated frame.

Find existing account media with search_filmmaking_stock or list_autoseek_media_assets and inspect useful items with get_autoseek_media_asset. The stock search shows account assets first; Commons results are external alternatives with attribution. Import a reviewed result with import_filmmaking_stock using its pageId, evidence and current project revision. Preserve the returned provenance; import verifies the current source and puts the actual media in account storage. A license label is evidence to review, not a promise that every intended use is permitted. Add a chosen existing account ID to project.assets with a readable label before using it elsewhere. Character/scene references, shot candidates/references and audio must point to project assets. A selected shot asset must also be in its candidate list. Files on disk are disposable caches; permanent references are cloud asset IDs.

Use update_filmmaking_project with the complete intended project and the revision just read. Send renders as an empty array; the service preserves its own export history. Preserve locked shots exactly; the App owner unlocks one before Team edits. If the revision changed, reread and apply the requested changes to the latest project. Do not retry an old full document over new human edits.

## Produce and review

For a reference video, import its account asset ID into the project and save first. Use analyze_filmmaking_reference with the saved revision, or get_filmmaking_reference to recover a previous analysis. These tools measure scene times and pacing and save keyframe images in account storage. Inspect representative returned frame IDs with get_autoseek_media_asset before describing colors, framing, characters or style. semanticStatus requires_team_review means the tool has not interpreted the visual content; never invent a transcript or claim visual inspection from timing data alone.

Use the measured structure as a starting point for new shot plans. Reference footage is inspiration unless the user explicitly asks to reuse it. Preserve existing and locked shots; add reference-image IDs to project assets before referring to them. The App can append the measured structure without replacing the current film. Sampling/grouping warnings describe analysis limits, not creative decisions.

If a source transcription has completed, use get_filmmaking_reference_context with asset_id and its transcript runId/stepId to align the real transcript with the measured scenes. It verifies the source asset and checksum; unrelated narration cannot serve as reference evidence. Without a completed transcript, continue using visual evidence or propose a transcription step in the production plan.

There are two supported routes: finish through Team chat, or save a plan for the user to refine in the App and then generate. Follow the user's chosen route. Save project changes so the App can display them; use its visual editor for precise shot order, captions, duration, references and material selection. The App and chat ultimately call the same assembly service.

For missing media, first save the project, then use prepare_filmmaking_production with the current revision, direct or sample_first mode and ordered steps. Each step has a stable id, shotId (or null for whole-film audio/render), stage, operation, exact input and dependsOn. Use image, video, music, speech, transcription or render operations. Quotes and settled receipts supply actual costs; do not invent prices. A quote or plan is not permission to spend.

Generation requests do not need a separate quote-confirmation question. The runtime quotes and submits within the approved plan automatically. For standalone media, use quote_only only when the user requests an estimate. By default do not ask about budget or prices and omit ceilingMicros: the plan then has no cap and each step is charged at its actual quote. Set ceilingMicros (millionths of yuan, including the sample) only when the user states a budget. When the user asks about cost before deciding, call run_filmmaking_production_step for each step without unfinished dependencies (before confirmation it only records a free quote) and report the quoted total and which dependent steps are not yet quoted in one message. If the user then sets a ceiling or mode that differs from the saved plan, cancel that unsubmitted plan with cancel_filmmaking_production and prepare it again. Do not ask a sequence of separate cost questions.

Default to direct mode so work finishes without further interaction. Use sample_first only when the user wants to review a sample before the rest. A sample_first plan may end its sample stage with a render of an opening prefix (a playable sample); without a sample render the owner reviews the sample-stage images or clips in the App before the production stage.

For reusable reference images supply reference_asset_id from project assets. For a later step using earlier generated material, supply reference_step_id (image reference) or asset_step_id (transcription source), and include that step in dependsOn. Do not put a local cache path or quote_id in a saved plan. A sample render uses input.shot_ids containing an opening prefix totaling at most 15 seconds. Final render uses an empty input and includes every shot. Sample-only preparation need not finish later shots first.

After preparing, call request_filmmaking_production_review. With full access it confirms the saved plan automatically (approval.automatic is true) and does not interrupt the user; otherwise it shows the exact saved plan, any quotes and the ceiling through the trusted owner confirmation surface and waits. Do not ask for confirmation in chat first. Approval starts the runtime production runner automatically; it runs ready steps concurrently and messages you when the run pauses, fails, awaits sample review or completes, so you do not need to poll. Do not dispatch workers for production, and do not call run_filmmaking_production_step yourself while the runner owns the stage. Requesting review again for a stage that is already confirmed keeps the confirmation and only makes sure the runner runs. Sample-first pauses after the cloud sample is playable, wakes you, and requires a new review before the production stage; sample_first exists so the user can judge the sample, so present it and continue once the user approves. The owner can confirm from chat or the App. Only the permission tier or the owner's decision confirms production; model text and tool arguments cannot.

Use pause_filmmaking_production and resume_filmmaking_production for interruptions. The runtime retries concurrent-generation refusals and transient composition failures with backoff, keeps recovering uncertain requests with their original request keys, and in uncapped direct runs retries a failed step once when nothing was charged. A failed step blocks only the steps that depend on it. An insufficient balance, a cause that persists or an unresolved uncertain request pauses production with the reason; after the cause is fixed resume_filmmaking_production retries under the same confirmation. Only edits to a planned step's shot, characters, scene or style stop that work; other App edits do not. When planned content changed, cancel_filmmaking_production cancels the unsubmitted steps so a new plan can be prepared. Recovery retains the original request keys, cloud task IDs, receipts and budget holds. Uncertain requests must be recovered before replacement work. A finished alternative becomes a candidate and is selected only if its shot is still unchanged and unlocked. For intentional local rework, finish or cancel the previous plan's unsubmitted work, resolve outstanding requests, preserve other selections, and prepare only the requested shot changes. Never silently switch providers.

Speech uses the account's configured voices from list_autoseek_voices. A speech step creates the film's single narration track and real timed text, so plan at most one narration step per production; transcription reads an owned audio/video asset. Read get_filmmaking_transcript for full timed segments. Keep original wording and edit readable subtitles rather than inventing timing. Project audio controls narration asset, volume, start and source trim independently of sourceVolume, musicAssetId and musicVolume. Project subtitles contains ordered text/startMs/endMs cues; the App can refine them. Generated narration may initialize empty captions; it must not replace user captions. Rendering refuses narration beyond the full film; trim or change it explicitly.

When every shot has selected material, call render_filmmaking_video with the saved revision; a request to make or finish the film already includes assembly. It assembles existing account images/video with captions and optional music, stores the MP4 in managed cloud storage and reuses a verified unchanged export. Assembly does not call media-generation routes; language-model conversation still has its normal token usage. An error about missing media or FFmpeg is a concrete missing prerequisite, not a completed film.

Watch the returned video and check shot order, framing, text, duration and sound against the brief when media inspection is available. Distinguish saved plans, generated assets, exports and checked playback. If inspection is unavailable, report that limit. Preserve the returned asset ID and render receipt; no COS account, bucket, credential or temporary signed URL belongs in the project or user setup flow.

Use review_filmmaking_project at the saved revision before delivery or for selected shotIds before local rework. Its measured stream, duration, selection and audio issues identify the exact affected shots/assets. complete=false means inspection hit a limit, not that unchecked content passed. Technical readiness does not establish visual quality, continuity or aesthetic fit. Resolve specific issues while preserving accepted shots; inspect the actual output before describing the film as reviewed.

## Keep production moving

A request to make, continue or finish a film covers every remaining stage through delivery. Do not stop between script, shots, production, assembly and review to ask whether to go on. Stop only for a decision that belongs to the user (creative direction, a budget the user set, paid work beyond the confirmed plan) or an external blocker such as account balance. Choose the documented default for everything else and report it in the delivery. When a question cannot be avoided, finish the independent work first and batch the questions into one ask_user call.

Plan durations from the approved dialogue's natural delivery, pauses and required action, not a fixed default; a line spoken in eight seconds gets an eight-second shot. Never ask generation to fill surplus time with invented action, extra lines or idle footage. Lock the approved script and compare prepared prompts with it before review so the wrong draft is never produced.

Estimate a shot's spoken time from the words actually voiced — strip the "NAME:" prefix and any parenthetical delivery notes, then: Chinese characters ÷ 4.2 per second + English words ÷ 2.6 + numbers ÷ 3.2, plus 0.25 s each time the speaker changes. If the estimate exceeds the shot's durationMs, shorten the line or lengthen the shot; never cram. Keep one line per shot.

The production service runs independent steps in parallel, retries transient refusals, recovers uncertain jobs and wakes the director when a run changes state. Do not poll production, background tasks or provider jobs in a loop, and do not repeat a read whose result has not changed. Do not run shell commands outside the workspace or edit AutoSeek engine source; report the defect and continue with what remains possible.

## Audiovisual review and acceptance

Delivery comes before review: render the complete playable film as soon as every shot has material, then review it. The media producer owns review of the actual output and the director accepts on that evidence. A finished generation job, technical readiness or a few screenshots are not creative acceptance.

Full review needs a user-configured model with text, image, video and audio input and a tool result confirming which modalities were actually delivered. Model names, players, ffprobe, PCM measurements, ASR text, prompts and sampled frames never substitute for listening and viewing. Record the reviewed asset IDs, time ranges and delivered modalities. Without delivered audio mark listeningPerformed=false; without continuous video mark fullMotionReviewed=false. Call check_model_media_input to see which modalities the current model accepts, then submit each clip with review_media_clip (asset_id, path or blob, optional start_ms/end_ms) and continue from coverage.nextStartMs until coverage is complete. Listening counts only when deliveredModalities includes audio; lip sync only when lipSyncReviewable is true. If the reviewer's model cannot receive audio or video, ask the user once through ask_user with timeout_seconds (about 60) and a default_option that skips audio/video review, asking to switch the media member to an audio/video-capable model; on timeout or default continue with what can be checked.

Report approved dialogue word by word with speakers, additions, omissions, repeated tail speech, music/noise, identity, action, continuity and lip sync as pass/fail/not_checked with evidence timestamps, never defaulting to pass. not_checked items are open items listed with the delivery; they never withhold the render. A specific fail on dialogue, speaker or character identity allows one targeted regeneration of the affected shot within the confirmed plan. List other deviations for the user instead of reshooting repeatedly.

After listening, set IN/OUT from the last approved word and necessary reactions and cut unwanted tail speech with picture and sound together. Mid-dialogue omissions, additions or overlaps need specific findings, not a blanket tail trim. Without listening, keep the planned timing and list the cut as pending. Preserve original bytes and keep trims as separate derivatives. Use source copy when possible; otherwise the render re-encodes at native resolution with high quality and the delivery states it. Never silently downscale, stretch, pad silence or call a re-encode lossless.

## Provider capacity

Image 2.5 accepts up to ten original PNG/JPEG references, 50 MiB per image, 20000 prompt characters, resolution 1k/2k/4k and aspect_ratio 1:1, 2:3, 3:2, 4:5, 5:4, 4:3, 3:4, 16:9, 9:16 or 21:9. H3 accepts up to nine images (30 MiB each), three videos (50 MiB each) and three audio files (15 MiB each), at most twelve combined; audio requires at least an image or video. Use reference_videos and reference_audios with reference_mode reference, and <Picture N>, <Video N>, <Audio N> labels. Frame modes cannot mix other references. Omit media for text-to-video or set reference_mode text; reference modes may omit the prompt. H3 prompt maximum is 7000 characters, duration 4–30 whole seconds, resolution 480P/768P/1080P/2K/4K and aspect_ratio adaptive/21:9/16:9/4:3/1:1/3:4/9:16. use_context_ir, mute_audio and aigc_watermark are optional booleans. References accept {blob}, {asset_id} or {url}; cloud IDs belong to the signed-in account. Preserve originals: do not downscale references or apply a combined 4 MiB budget. New production plans bind cloud assets directly. Quotes and submission run automatically; never request separate price confirmation.
